Respect the designer’s licence

Many models are shared under Creative Commons licences. Some allow remixes and some don’t, and some are for non-commercial use only. Check the licence on the model’s page, credit the designer, and don’t share or sell a remix the licence doesn’t allow.

Frequently asked questions

How do I edit an STL file online for free?

Open twyk in your browser, sign in with Google, and import the STL (up to 64 MB). You can resize it, add text or holes, cut it, take it apart with Split, or push or pull its faces. Then export it as STL or 3MF. twyk is free.

Can I modify a model I downloaded from Thingiverse, Printables or MakerWorld?

Yes, if the file is STL, 3MF or SVG, which covers most downloads from these sites. Download it, import it into twyk, and tweak it. Check the model’s licence first: some allow remixes and some don’t.

What file types can twyk import?

STL, 3MF and SVG files, one at a time, up to 64 MB. A 3MF comes in as separate objects with their colours, and an SVG becomes a flat solid. OBJ files can’t be imported, so convert them to STL first.

Will a 3MF from MakerWorld keep its colours?

A 3MF comes into twyk as separate objects with their colours. Print settings aren’t brought in; you set those in your slicer as usual.

Can I change the shape of an imported STL?

An STL is a mesh, so it arrives as one solid. You can resize it, cut it, add to it, cut holes into it, push or pull its flat faces, and use Split to separate its parts or turn pieces into editable shapes where possible.

Is it allowed to remix models I download?

It depends on the licence the designer chose. Many models use Creative Commons licences; some allow remixes and some don’t, and some are for non-commercial use only. Check the licence on the model’s page and credit the designer.
