twyk vs Layerling

Layerling and twyk are both free, browser-based tools for designing 3D prints, and both aim at people who want more than Tinkercad. They grow in different directions. Layerling leans toward classic CAD: fillets on any edge, standard threads, sketches you extrude or revolve, and STEP export, with designs kept in your browser. twyk leans toward speed and shape: AI twyk builds models from a description or photo, Sculpt and Formula make organic and mathematical shapes, and Flex and Gaps test whether a print will work.

✓ Choose twyk if…

  • You want to describe a part, or start from a photo, and let AI build the first version for free.
  • You want organic shapes (Sculpt) or mathematical ones like lattices and knots (Formula).
  • You want your models saved to an account and available on any computer.
  • You want to test flexible prints and check print-in-place clearances before printing.

Choose Layerling if…

  • You need fillets on any edge or real, standard threads for screws and nuts.
  • You need STEP export or workshop drawings for machining.
  • You want open-source software you can self-host, with no account at all.

Moving from Layerling to twyk

Export from Layerling as STL or 3MF and import into twyk. A 3MF keeps its separate objects and colours. From there you can cut, add to, or ask AI twyk to change the model. See importing and exporting, or how to bring in models from any site.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between twyk and Layerling?

Both are free browser tools for designing 3D prints. Layerling focuses on CAD-style precision such as fillets on any edge, standard threads, sketches and STEP export, with designs stored in your browser. twyk focuses on AI twyk, which builds from a description or photo, organic and formula-based shaping, and print tests like Flex and Gaps, with models saved to your account.

Does Layerling have AI?

Layerling can connect to your own AI assistant through an MCP interface, but only when you run your own copy; the connection is off on layerling.com. twyk's AI twyk is built in and free.

Can twyk make threads?

twyk has no dedicated thread tool. You can make springs and coils with Formula, and if you need standard metric or imperial threads, Layerling is the stronger choice.

Is twyk open source?

No. Layerling is open source and self-hostable; twyk is a free hosted app you sign into with Google.
