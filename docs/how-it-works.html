From an idea to a printed part

Describe what you want and let AI build the first version, or start from a blank workplane. Either way you finish in the same precise editor, right in your browser. Here is the whole workflow, start to finish.

1 Describe it

Start with a sentence or a photo

Type what you want to make, or start from a photo of the thing you need. twyk generates a 3D model that opens straight in the editor, ready to change.

  • Be specific about fit: “a stand for my Arctis Nova Pro headset, 260 mm tall”
  • Say what it’s for: desk edge, wall mount, fits a Raspberry Pi 5
  • Not quite right? Ask again and pick the version you like

2 Make it fit

Adjust it in plain words

Tell the editor what to change, and it makes the exact edit for you:

  • “Make the saddle 72 mm wide”
  • “Add two 4 mm screw holes, 55 mm apart”
  • “Put NOVA on the base, 8 mm tall”

Ask for 72 mm and you get 72.00 mm. You can always fine-tune by hand with the tools below.

3 Build with shapes

Or build it yourself from the shape library

Prefer to start from scratch, or want to add parts to a generated model? Click a shape to drop it on the workplane. The library goes far past the basics, organised into ten categories:

  • Basic & Curved for boxes, cylinders, cones and smooth solids
  • Polyhedra & Profiles for dice, prisms and extrusion outlines
  • Symbols, Parts, Home, Building and Fun for ready-made pieces
  • Text for names, labels and embossing

There is also a search box when you know what you're after.

4 Dial in the details

Real dimensions, not guesswork

Select any shape and the properties panel gives you exact control:

  • Solid or Hole — flip a shape into a cutter to carve cavities and slots
  • Size in mm on all three axes, with keep-aspect and resize %
  • Position and rotation typed in exactly, or dragged with handles
  • Colour per shape, ready for multi-colour printing

Parts fit the first time because you told them exactly how big to be.

5 Combine and edit

One toolbar for the whole build

Move, rotate and resize with direct handles, then shape the design with the tools that do the heavy lifting:

  • Group shapes to move them as one, Merge to fuse them into a single solid
  • Align on any axis and Duplicate for repeats
  • Split and Cut to take a design apart, Push face to pull a single face in or out

And the "Saved" badge on the left is doing exactly what it says: your work saves as you go.

6 Export or publish

Straight to your slicer, or straight to the world

When the design is done, export it in the format your workflow needs:

  • Export 3MF — keeps the separate objects, ideal for multi-colour printing
  • Export STL — one mesh that opens in any slicer or CAD tool

Or publish it to twyk.me instead: your model gets a live 3D page, a gallery, print files and a place on your creator profile — and anyone can open it back up and remix it, by hand or with AI, with credit linking back to you.
