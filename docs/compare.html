twyk vs the alternatives

Tinkercad, Layerling, 3D Slash, SketchUp for Web and Onshape are all good tools, built for different people. twyk is built for one job: getting from “I need this” to a part that prints. You build it from real, editable shapes, size it to the millimetre and check it will print. Here’s how they compare, and when another tool is the better pick.

Which one should you pick?

✓ twyk, if…

  • You want something as easy as Tinkercad, with exact millimetres and every step undoable.
  • You want shapes beyond blocks: melt shapes together with Sculpt, make springs and lattices with Formula, bend and twist.
  • You print things that have to work: test a flexible part before printing, and check moving parts won’t fuse.

Tinkercad, if…

  • You’re teaching a class and want its mature classroom tools and lesson library.
  • You also want electronics simulation (Circuits) or block-based code (Codeblocks).

Layerling, if…

  • You need fillets and chamfers on any edge, or real metric and imperial threads.
  • You want STEP export, or to keep designs on your own computer with no account.

3D Slash, if…

  • You’re introducing young makers to 3D, and block-by-block building feels natural to them.

SketchUp for Web, if…

  • You’re modelling rooms, buildings or furniture layouts, where push/pull on faces shines.

Onshape, if…

  • You’re designing mechanical assemblies that need full parametric history and constraints, and you’re ready for a steeper learning curve.

Coming from another tool

twyk imports STL, 3MF and SVG files, so you can bring a design from Tinkercad, Layerling, 3D Slash, or a model you downloaded from Thingiverse, Printables or MakerWorld, as a starting point (here’s how). Then use Split to take an imported model apart into editable shapes, or Push face to adjust it. See importing and exporting, or everything twyk can do.
