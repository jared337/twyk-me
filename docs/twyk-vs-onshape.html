twyk vs Onshape

Onshape is professional parametric CAD: you sketch with dimensions and constraints, build up a feature history, and design whole mechanical assemblies. It's powerful, with a learning curve to match, and on the free plan your documents are public and for non-commercial use. twyk is for getting printable parts made quickly: build it from editable shapes, size it to the millimetre, and check it prints. Your models stay private.

✓ Choose twyk if…

  • You want printable parts without learning professional CAD.
  • You want your models private, for free.
  • You want quick checks that a flexible or print-in-place part will work.

Choose Onshape if…

  • You’re designing mechanical assemblies that need constraints, mates and a full feature history.
  • You need STEP output and professional CAD workflows.

Moving from Onshape to twyk

Export a part from Onshape as STL and import it into twyk, for example to personalise it or add a mount. Onshape remains the better home for the parametric source. See importing and exporting, or how to bring in models from any site.

What twyk adds

  • 80+ shapes and 48 fonts, each with settings you can change later. See the shapes
  • Exact sizes in millimetres or inches, a ruler for gaps, and X-ray for hidden parts. See the precision tools
  • Sculpt, Formula and Bend make organic, mathematical and curved shapes. See the shaping tools
  • Flex and Gaps test flexible parts and print-in-place clearances before you print. See the print checks

Frequently asked questions

Is twyk an Onshape alternative?

For many 3D printing projects, yes: twyk is easier, free and private, with print checks built in. For mechanical assemblies that need constraints and full parametric history, Onshape is the better tool.

Are Onshape free files public?

Yes. Documents created on Onshape's free plan are publicly accessible, and the plan is for non-commercial use. twyk models are private until you choose to share them.

Is twyk parametric?

Partly. Every twyk shape keeps its own settings, such as size, radius or number of teeth, which you can change later. twyk doesn't have sketch constraints or a feature history.
