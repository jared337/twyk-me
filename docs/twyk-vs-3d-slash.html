twyk vs 3D Slash

3D Slash and twyk are both easy ways into 3D design, but they build in very different ways. 3D Slash works like Minecraft: you add and carve away blocks, which feels natural to young makers. twyk works with smooth shapes and holes measured in millimetres. twyk is free with no paid tier; 3D Slash has a free tier with queued downloads and paid plans for more.

✓ Choose twyk if…

  • You want smooth shapes and exact millimetre sizes for parts that fit.
  • You want everything free, with no queues or paid tiers.
  • You print functional parts and want to test them before printing.

Choose 3D Slash if…

  • You’re introducing young makers to 3D, and block-by-block building feels natural to them.
  • You want an offline app.

Moving from 3D Slash to twyk

Export from 3D Slash as STL and import it into twyk. Then smooth things out by adding shapes, cut it, or add holes and mounts. See importing and exporting, or how to bring in models from any site.

What twyk adds

  • 80+ shapes and 48 fonts, each with settings you can change later. See the shapes
  • Exact sizes in millimetres or inches, a ruler for gaps, and X-ray for hidden parts. See the precision tools
  • Sculpt, Formula and Bend make organic, mathematical and curved shapes. See the shaping tools
  • Flex and Gaps test flexible parts and print-in-place clearances before you print. See the print checks

Frequently asked questions

Is twyk easier than 3D Slash?

3D Slash's block-building has the gentlest start, which is why it suits young children. twyk is also designed to be easy: drop shapes on a workplane and type the sizes you want.

Is twyk free?

Yes, completely, with no plans, payments or download queues. 3D Slash has a free tier, with paid plans from $2 a month for immediate downloads, more colours and the offline app.

Can I bring a 3D Slash model into twyk?

Yes. Export it as STL and import it into twyk, up to 64 MB.
